Signature Exercise

The Problem Behind the Problem

Sixty minutes. Standing triads. Four timed rounds. The goal is system structure, not a tidy root cause. Round 4 is what keeps this from becoming either an academic exercise or a complaint session.

Four-Round Structured Diagnostic Exercise

Round 1 · 15 min

Presenting Problem

Partners ask "why does that persist" — three times, not five. The goal is system structure, not a tidy root cause.

15 minutes
Round 2 · 20 min

Actors & Flows

20 minutes
Round 3 · 15 min

Locate & Reframe

15 minutes
Round 4 · 10 min

The Mandate Test

Name what is fixed and what is discretionary. The reframe must live in the discretionary space to survive the round.

System Loop Analysis: The Reading Proficiency Illustration

Presenting Problem: Reading materials arrive late due to a fiscal, rather than instructional, procurement calendar.

The Balancing Loop: Teachers improvise substitutes. This holds outcomes just high enough that the procurement timing never registers as a critical failure at the central office.

The Leverage Placement: Instead of procuring more materials (Stocks), the intervention moves one rung up — creating a short-cycle information flow on school-level readiness to inform the timing decision before it locks.

Exercise Timer

5-minute structured interview panel demonstration

5:00
1
Name the Problem — The Presenting Symptom
0:00 – 0:45
Facilitator (Interviewer) "Think of a recurring problem in your PAP — one that has persisted despite interventions. State it in one sentence. What is the symptom you keep seeing?"
Participant (Officer) "Reading proficiency in our target schools remains below the standard the program was funded to raise it to, despite three years of supplementary material distribution."
Facilitator Note Notice: the participant stated an outcome gap, not a problem. The symptom is "below standard." The question is: what system structure produces this symptom repeatedly?
2
Three Times Why — Revealing Non-Linear Relationships
0:45 – 2:15
Facilitator — Why #1 "Why does reading proficiency remain low despite materials being distributed?"
Participant "Because the materials arrive late — usually three months into the school year. Teachers have already settled into their own improvised routines by then."
Facilitator — Why #2 "Why do materials arrive late? Is it a logistics failure, or something structural?"
Participant "It's the procurement calendar. The cycle is keyed to the fiscal year, not the instructional calendar. The request locks in January for a September delivery — but schools need them in June."
Facilitator — Why #3 "And why hasn't anyone connected school readiness timing to the procurement decision? What's the missing link?"
Participant "Because nothing carries school-level readiness data back to the central office before the timing decision locks. The information flow doesn't exist."
Non-Linear Relationship Revealed This is not a linear chain (A→B→C). It's a system with feedback loops:

Balancing Loop: Teachers improvise → outcomes held "just high enough" → gap masked from center → timing deprioritized → materials still late → teachers keep improvising.

Reinforcing Loop: Late arrival → low uptake → weak demand signal → timing deprioritized → later arrival next cycle.

The problem persists because the system is structured to absorb the failure.
3
Locate the Intervention — Apply Leverage Points
2:15 – 3:45
Facilitator "Where is the current intervention on the leverage ladder? What rung are you operating on?"
Participant "We're at the bottom — procuring more materials (Stocks) and adjusting timelines (Parameters). We've been trying to fix this with more of the same."
Facilitator "Now move exactly ONE rung up. Not to the top — one rung. What changes?"
Participant "One rung up is Information Flows. Instead of procuring more materials, we create a short-cycle read on school-level readiness — a signal that reaches the procurement decision before it locks. We don't change what we buy. We change when the decision is informed."
Leverage Point Applied Current (Rung 1–2): More materials, adjusted timelines — parameters and stocks.
Advanced (Rung 4): Create an information flow that carries school readiness data to the timing decision point before it closes.

This is the same budget. Same appropriation. Same coverage. But the structure of information changes — and with it, the decision quality.
4
The Mandate Test — Does It Survive Reality?
3:45 – 5:00
Facilitator "Last question. Name what is fixed by law or appropriation — and what is discretionary in your design. Does your reframe survive inside the discretionary space?"
Participant "Fixed: the appropriation amount, the material specifications, the coverage targets. Discretionary: when the procurement request is initiated, how teacher training is sequenced, and whether a feedback mechanism exists at all. My information flow intervention lives entirely in the discretionary space."
Demonstration Complete What the panel observed:

1. Problem analysis: Moved from symptom ("low proficiency") to system structure (misaligned calendars, masked gaps).
2. Non-linear relationships: Revealed balancing and reinforcing loops that explain why the problem persists despite interventions.
3. Leverage points: Located current intervention at Rung 1–2, moved to Rung 4 (Information Flows) — one rung, not to the top.
4. Mandate test: Confirmed the reframe survives within discretionary authority.

This is the part of the design that a slide cannot show you. You are hiring a facilitator, not a document.

Why Sustainability Gets More Time, Not Less

PAPs managed under Contract of Service arrangements carry the sharpest continuity risk in the Department, because program memory is tied to contract cycles. Treating transition as a live design problem is more urgent here than in the permanent batches.

Named trade-off: return-on-investment analysis becomes a worked example rather than individual application. If the Bureau prefers the reverse, the compression can be reallocated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Everything you need to know about the Advanced Program Management LDI.

Who is this program designed for?
This LDI is designed for Salary Grade 15 and above officers at the Department of Education Central Office who are already facilitating or managing live Programs, Activities, and Projects (PAPs). These are not learners acquiring a first framework — the advanced increment is judgment under complexity, not more technique.
What is the "Live PAP Methodology"?
Every participant enrols one real PAP they currently manage, and works it through all modules across the four days. This replaces generic case studies with live, personally accountable program work.
What is the Leverage Ladder?
A hierarchy of intervention points from parameters (lowest) to system goals (highest). Standard public sector programs cluster at the two lowest rungs. Advanced program management intervenes higher up the ladder, targeting information flows, rules, or structure.
Why might post-assessment scores go down?
A participant who confidently agreed on Day 0 that they could explain their causal chain, and who disagrees on Day 4, has not deteriorated — they have learned what a causal chain actually requires. On problem-definition and results-logic items, a modest decline in self-assessment is the expected signature of the intervention working.
What is the "Fixes That Fail" exercise?
A structured six-step exercise where participants map a recurring problem using causal loop diagrams. They identify the problem symptom, the quick fix, intended and unintended consequences, key variables, behavior-over-time patterns, and finally diagram both the balancing loop (quick fix) and the reinforcing loop (unintended consequences) that explains why the problem persists despite repeated interventions.
How does the 5-minute interview panel demonstration work?
The facilitator demonstrates live problem analysis with a participant in four phases: (1) naming the presenting symptom, (2) asking "why" three times to reveal non-linear feedback loops, (3) locating the current intervention on the leverage ladder and moving one rung higher, and (4) applying the mandate test to confirm the reframe survives within discretionary authority. This shows the panel exactly how the facilitator thinks — not just what slides they use.
